1 Volume of a right rectangular prism Band B1MCQ

What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the right rectangular prism shown?

  1. A) 16
  2. B) 40
  3. C) 120
  4. D) 158
Answer & solution

Answer: C) 120

V = lwh = 8 · 5 · 3 = 120 cubic centimeters. The volume formula is on the reference sheet.

  • A) 16 adds the three edge lengths (8 + 5 + 3).
  • B) 40 stops at the base area (8 · 5).
  • D) 158 computes the surface area, 2(40 + 24 + 15).

RP67: single reference-sheet formula (V = lwh), one forward substitution — the Band 1 descriptor verbatim, so B1 students clear 0.67.

2 Parallel lines cut by a transversal Band B2MCQ

In the figure, line ℓ is parallel to line m. What is the value of x?

  1. A) 28
  2. B) 62
  3. C) 72
  4. D) 118
Answer & solution

Answer: B) 62

The 118° angle and the x° angle are same-side (co-interior) angles between the parallel lines, so they are supplementary: x = 180 − 118 = 62.

  • A) 28 subtracts from 90 instead of 180 (complement error: 118 − 90).
  • C) 72 is the arithmetic slip 190 − 118.
  • D) 118 assumes same-side interior angles are congruent rather than supplementary.

RP67: one basic parallel-lines-transversal theorem, single forward step — exactly the Band 2 transversal gate; B1 students miss the supplementary-vs-congruent call.

3 Triangle angle sum / exterior angle Band B3MCQ

In the figure, side BC of triangle ABC is extended to point D. What is the value of x?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

  1. A) 38
  2. B) 65
  3. C) 77
  4. D) 103
Answer & solution

Answer: C) 77

Angle ACB and the 142° angle form a linear pair, so m∠ACB = 180 − 142 = 38°. By the triangle angle sum theorem, x = 180 − 65 − 38 = 77. (Equivalently, the exterior angle theorem gives 142 = 65 + x.)

  • A) 38 stops after the linear pair (that is angle ACB, not x).
  • B) 65 assumes the two remote interior angles are equal.
  • D) 103 is the supplement of the correct answer (180 − 77).

RP67: two-link forward chain of angle theorems (linear pair → triangle angle sum) — Band 3's angle-theorem gate; B2 students typically drop a link.

4 Special right triangle (30–60–90) Band B4SPR

In right triangle ABC shown, what is the value of x?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & solution

Answer: 10

In a 30–60–90 triangle the sides opposite the 30°, 60°, and 90° angles are s, s√3, and 2s. Leg AC is opposite the 60° angle at B, so s√3 = 5√3, giving s = 5. The hypotenuse is x = 2s = 10. Check: BC = 5 and 5² + (5√3)² = 25 + 75 = 100 = 10². ✓

RP67: reference-sheet special-right-triangle fact, but the student must match 5√3 to the s√3 leg and invert to the hypotenuse — Band 4's special-right gate (SPR removes answer-matching help).

5 Equation of a circle from its graph Band B5MCQ

The graph of a circle in the xy-plane is shown. Which of the following equations defines the circle?

  1. A) (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 5
  2. B) (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25
  3. C) (x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 5
  4. D) (x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25
Answer & solution

Answer: B) (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25

From the graph the center is (3, −2) and the circle passes through (7, 1). Then r² = (7 − 3)² + (1 − (−2))² = 16 + 9 = 25, so the equation is (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.

  • A) uses r = 5 on the right side instead of r² = 25.
  • C) makes both the sign error and the r-vs-r² error.
  • D) flips the signs of the center coordinates ((x − h) written as (x + h)).

RP67: off-sheet standard-form circle equation plus reading center/point from the graph and computing r² — Band 5's write-the-circle-equation gate.

6 Multistep perimeter of a composite figure Band B5MCQ

The figure shows the floor plan of a patio formed by a rectangle and a semicircle whose diameter is a side of the rectangle. What is the perimeter, in meters, of the patio?

  1. A) 32 + 4π
  2. B) 40 + 4π
  3. C) 32 + 8π
  4. D) 96 + 8π
Answer & solution

Answer: A) 32 + 4π

The boundary consists of the top and bottom sides (12 + 12), the left side (8), and the semicircular arc. The semicircle has diameter 8, so radius 4 and arc length ½(2π · 4) = 4π. The dashed 8 m segment is interior, not part of the boundary. Perimeter = 12 + 12 + 8 + 4π = 32 + 4π ≈ 44.57 m.

  • B) 40 + 4π wrongly includes the interior 8 m diameter segment.
  • C) 32 + 8π uses the full circle's circumference instead of half.
  • D) 96 + 8π computes the area (96 + 8π), not the perimeter.

RP67: multistep perimeter chaining two sheet formulas (rectangle sides + half a circumference) while suppressing the interior edge — Band 5's multistep area/perimeter gate.

7 Triangle similarity (parallel-line proportionality) Band B6SPR

In triangle ABC shown, segment DE is parallel to side BC. What is the length of segment BC?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & solution

Answer: 15

Since DE ∥ BC, △ADE ~ △ABC (AA). The scale factor uses the whole side: AB = AD + DB = 6 + 4 = 10, so AB/AD = 10/6. Then BC = DE · (10/6) = 9 · 5/3 = 15. (The classic trap, BC = 9 · 4/6 = 6, uses the part DB instead of the whole AB.)

RP67: similarity with a hidden scale factor — must compose AB = AD + DB, then inverse-solve the proportion; beyond Band 4's direct scale-factor use, so B6.

8 Circle theorems: tangents, central angles, arcs Band B7MCQ

In the figure, segments PA and PB are tangent to the circle with center O at points A and B, respectively. What is the measure, in degrees, of minor arc AB?

  1. A) 40
  2. B) 70
  3. C) 140
  4. D) 220
Answer & solution

Answer: C) 140

A radius drawn to a point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent, so m∠OAP = m∠OBP = 90°. In quadrilateral PAOB the angles sum to 360°, so m∠AOB = 360 − 90 − 90 − 40 = 140°. A minor arc has the same measure as its central angle, so arc AB = 140°.

  • A) 40 assumes the arc equals the tangent-tangent angle at P.
  • B) 70 halves the central angle (inscribed-angle confusion).
  • D) 220 reports the major arc instead of the minor arc.

RP67: three-link off-sheet chain — tangent ⊥ radius recall, quadrilateral angle sum, then arc = central angle — Band 7's circles-and-parts-of-circles gate.

9 Volume of a composite solid (cylinder + hemisphere) Band B7SPR

A water storage tank is formed by a right circular cylinder and a hemisphere, as shown. The volume of the tank is kπ cubic meters, where k is a constant. What is the value of k?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & solution

Answer: 81

The hemisphere and cylinder share radius 3 m. The hemisphere adds 3 m of height, so the cylinder's height is 10 − 3 = 7 m. Cylinder: πr²h = π(9)(7) = 63π. Hemisphere: ½ · (4/3)πr³ = (2/3)π(27) = 18π. Total volume = 63π + 18π = 81π cubic meters, so k = 81.

RP67: modeled composite solid — decompose the object, adapt the sheet sphere formula to a hemisphere, and inverse-extract the cylinder height from the total height — Band 7's modeled-solids gate.

10 Right-triangle trig via similarity (altitude to hypotenuse) Band B7SPR

Triangle ABC shown has a right angle at C. Point D lies on AB, and segment CD is perpendicular to segment AB. What is the value of tan(∠DCB)?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & solution

Answer: 3/4 (equivalently .75)

In right triangle CDB, ∠DCB is complementary to ∠B. In right triangle ACB, ∠A is also complementary to ∠B. Therefore ∠DCB ≅ ∠A (equivalently, △CDB ~ △ACB). So tan(∠DCB) = tan A = BC/AC = 6/8 = 3/4. Independent check: with C = (0, 0), A = (8, 0), B = (0, 6), the foot of the altitude is D = (72/25, 96/25), and the angle between rays CD and CB has tangent exactly 0.75. ✓

RP67: the target angle's triangle has no given sides — the ratio is reachable only by recognizing angle congruence through similarity — Band 7's "use similarity to calculate values of trigonometric ratios" gate.
