1 Band B1 MCQ Volume of a right rectangular prism

What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the right rectangular prism shown?

  1. A16
  2. B40
  3. C120
  4. D158
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — 120

V = ℓwh = 8 · 5 · 3 = 120 cubic centimeters. All three edge lengths are read directly off the figure.

Distractors: A sums the three edges (8 + 5 + 3); B is the base area only (8 · 5); D is the surface area, 2(40 + 24 + 15) = 158.

RP67 (B1): On-sheet formula (V = ℓwh is on the reference sheet), single forward substitution of labeled edges — the lowest-band volume gate; B1 students clear it at ≥ 0.67.

2 Band B2 MCQ Parallel lines cut by a transversal

In the figure, line ℓ is parallel to line m. What is the value of x?

  1. A28
  2. B62
  3. C72
  4. D118
Answer & Solution

Answer: B — 62

The 118° angle and the x° angle are same-side (co-interior) angles between the parallel lines, so they are supplementary: x = 180 − 118 = 62.

Distractors: D treats the pair as alternate interior (congruent) angles; A subtracts from 90 instead of 180 (118 − 90 = 28); C is a common arithmetic slip on 180 − 118.

RP67 (B2): One transversal theorem plus one subtraction — the explicit B2 parallel-lines gate; no formula sheet, no chain.

3 Band B3 SPR Triangle angle sum · vertical angles

In the figure, two lines intersect at the top vertex of a triangle whose other two angles measure 52° and 61°, as shown. What is the value of x?

Answer & Solution

Answer: 67

The apex angle of the triangle measures 180 − 52 − 61 = 67°. The angle marked x° is the vertical angle to the apex angle formed by the two intersecting lines, so x = 67.

RP67 (B3): A two-theorem chain (triangle angle sum, then vertical angles), all off the reference sheet — the first band that reliably clears multi-theorem angle chains is B3.

4 Band B4 MCQ 30°-60°-90° special right triangle

In right triangle ABC shown, what is the value of x?

  1. A7
  2. B7√2
  3. C7√3
  4. D14√3
Answer & Solution

Answer: A — 7

Angle A = 60° and angle C = 90°, so angle B = 30°. Side x = AC is opposite the 30° angle, and in a 30°-60°-90° triangle the side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse: x = 14 ÷ 2 = 7.

Distractors: C picks the leg opposite 60° (7√3); B applies the 45°-45°-90° ratio; D multiplies the hypotenuse by √3 instead of halving it.

RP67 (B4): The 30-60-90 ratios are on the reference sheet, but selecting which leg is opposite 30° (after inferring the unmarked 30° angle) is the B4 special-right-triangle gate.

5 Band B5 MCQ Equation of a circle in the xy-plane

The graph of a circle in the xy-plane is shown. Which of the following is an equation of the circle?

  1. A(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25
  2. B(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 5
  3. C(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25
  4. D(x − 7)² + (y − 1)² = 25
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25

The center is (3, −2) and the circle passes through (7, 1), so r² = (7 − 3)² + (1 − (−2))² = 16 + 9 = 25. Standard form gives (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.

Distractors: A flips the signs of the center; B writes r instead of r²; D uses the on-circle point as the center.

RP67 (B5): Radius must be constructed from center + on-circle point (distance formula) and then encoded in standard form — exactly the circle-equation capability the rubric places at B5.

6 Band B6 SPR Volume of a cylinder — solve for a dimension

The right circular cylinder shown has a volume of 288π cubic centimeters. Based on the figure, what is the value of h? (Lengths shown are in centimeters.)

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & Solution

Answer: 18

V = πr²h, so 288π = π(4)²h = 16πh. Dividing both sides by 16π gives h = 288 ⁄ 16 = 18.

RP67 (B6): The formula πr²h is on the sheet, but it must be run in the inverse direction — recovering a dimension from a given volume is the B6 gate, one step past B5's forward formula use.

7 Band B6 MCQ Right-triangle trigonometric ratio

In right triangle ABC shown, what is the value of tan A?

  1. A20⁄29
  2. B21⁄29
  3. C21⁄20
  4. D20⁄21
Answer & Solution

Answer: D — 20⁄21

From vertex A, the opposite leg is BC = 20 and the adjacent leg is AC = 21 (and indeed 20² + 21² = 841 = 29²). So tan A = opposite ⁄ adjacent = 20⁄21.

Distractors: A is sin A; B is cos A; C is the reciprocal (tan of angle B).

RP67 (B6): SOH-CAH-TOA is off-sheet recall, and opposite vs. adjacent must be identified from the figure relative to vertex A — the rubric's B6 right-triangle-trig gate.

8 Band B7 SPR Circle theorems — tangent ⊥ radius

In the figure, segment PA is tangent to the circle with center O at point A. What is the length of radius OA?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & Solution

Answer: 10

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency, so triangle OAP is right-angled at A with hypotenuse OP = 26. Then OA = √(26² − 24²) = √(676 − 576) = √100 = 10.

RP67 (B7): The tangent-perpendicular-to-radius theorem is pure off-sheet recall and must be chained into an inverse Pythagorean step — circle-theorem chains are the rubric's B7 territory.

9 Band B7 SPR Similar right triangles — altitude to the hypotenuse

In triangle ABC shown, angle ABC is a right angle, and BD is perpendicular to AC. What is the length of BD?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & Solution

Answer: 6

The altitude to the hypotenuse creates triangles ADB and BDC that are similar to each other (each is similar to triangle ABC). From △ADB ∼ △BDC: AD⁄BD = BD⁄DC, so BD² = AD · DC = 4 · 9 = 36, giving BD = 6.

RP67 (B7): Requires recognizing the three-similar-triangles structure hidden in the figure and extracting the geometric-mean proportion — a multi-step similarity chain, the rubric's B7 similar-triangles gate.

10 Band B7 MCQ Volume of a composite solid (3-D modeling)

The figure shows a model of a grain silo consisting of a right circular cylinder topped by a right circular cone that shares its base with the cylinder. Based on the figure, what is the volume, in cubic feet, of the silo?

  1. A72π
  2. B90π
  3. C108π
  4. D144π
Answer & Solution

Answer: B — 90π

The cylinder's height is not given directly: it is 14 − 6 = 8 ft. Then V = πr²h + ⅓πr²hcone = π(3²)(8) + ⅓π(3²)(6) = 72π + 18π = 90π cubic feet.

Distractors: A is the cylinder alone; C treats the cone as a cylinder (72π + 36π); D uses the full 14 ft as the cylinder height and then adds the cone (126π + 18π).

RP67 (B7): Decomposing a modeled solid, deriving the hidden cylinder height (14 − 6), and chaining two sheet formulas — multi-formula 3-D modeling is the rubric's B7 gate.
