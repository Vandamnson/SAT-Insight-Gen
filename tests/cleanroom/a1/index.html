Problem 1 Band B1Multiple Choice Volume of a right rectangular prism

What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the right rectangular prism shown?

  1. A)12
  2. B)20
  3. C)60
  4. D)94
Answer & Solution

Answer: C) 60

The reference-sheet formula gives V = ℓ·w·h = 5 · 4 · 3 = 60 cubic centimeters. All three edge lengths are read directly from the figure.

Distractors: A) adds the three edges (5+4+3); B) multiplies only two dimensions (5·4); D) is the surface area, 2(20+15+12) = 94.

RP67 — B1: single on-sheet formula (V = ℓwh), one forward substitution, no chain; this is the Band-1 descriptor verbatim.

  1. A)34
  2. B)56
  3. C)66

Answer & Solution

Answer: B) 56

The 124° angle and the x° angle are same-side (co-interior) angles between the parallel lines, so they are supplementary: x = 180 − 124 = 56.

Problem 4 Band B4Multiple Choice Triangle similarity — parallel segment

In triangle ABC shown, point D lies on side AB, point E lies on side AC, and segment DE is parallel to segment BC. If AD = 6, DB = 4, and DE = 9, what is the length of BC?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

  1. A)6
  2. B)13
  3. C)15
  4. D)22.5
Answer & Solution

Answer: C) 15

Because DE ∥ BC, triangles ADE and ABC are similar (AA). The figure shows D between A and B, so AB = AD + DB = 10. Then BC = DE · (AB/AD) = 9 · (10/6) = 15.

Distractors: A) uses the ratio DB/AD (9 · 4/6); B) adds DB to DE (additive misconception, 9 + 4); D) uses AB/DB instead of AB/AD (9 · 10/4).

RP67 — B4: one similarity setup plus one forward proportion; the scale-factor gate (whole side AB vs. sub-segment DB) is exactly the B4 descriptor.
Problem 5 Band B5Multiple Choice Equation of a circle in the xy-plane

The circle shown in the xy-plane has center (3, −2), and the point (7, 1) lies on the circle. Which of the following is an equation of the circle?

  1. A)(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25
  2. B)(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25
  3. C)(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 5
  4. D)(x − 7)² + (y − 1)² = 25
Answer & Solution

Answer: A) (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25

The radius is the distance from (3, −2) to (7, 1): r² = (7 − 3)² + (1 − (−2))² = 16 + 9 = 25. The standard form with center (h, k) = (3, −2) gives (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.

Distractors: B) flips the signs of the center; C) puts r = 5 rather than r² = 25 on the right side; D) uses the on-circle point as the center.

RP67 — B5: off-sheet recall of the circle standard form chained with one distance computation (center + point → equation) — the B5 descriptor verbatim.

Problem 7 Band B5Multiple Choice Composite area (multistep)

The figure shows the design of a window consisting of a rectangle and a semicircle whose diameter is the top side of the rectangle. Based on the dimensions in the figure, what is the area, in square centimeters, of the window?

  1. A)2,400 + 20π
  2. B)2,400 + 200π
  3. C)2,400 + 400π
  4. D)2,400 + 800π
Answer & Solution

Answer: B) 2,400 + 200π

Rectangle: 40 · 60 = 2,400. The semicircle's diameter is 40, so its radius is 20, and its area is ½·π·20² = 200π. Total area: 2,400 + 200π square centimeters.

Distractors: A) uses half the circumference (½ · 2π · 20) instead of area; C) uses a full circle; D) uses the 40-cm diameter as the radius.

RP67 — B5: multistep composite-area decomposition with on-sheet formulas; the chain (decompose, halve diameter, halve circle) rather than recall is the gate — the B5 multistep-area descriptor.
Problem 8 Band B7Student-Produced Response Circle theorems — tangent and radius

In the figure, segment PA is tangent to the circle with center O at point A, and point B is the intersection of segment PO with the circle. If PA = 24 and PO = 26, what is the length of PB?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

Enter your answer in the grid.

Answer & Solution

Answer: 16

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency, so triangle OAP has a right angle at A. Then OA = √(PO² − PA²) = √(676 − 576) = √100 = 10, so the radius is 10. Since B lies on segment PO and on the circle, OB = 10, so PB = PO − OB = 26 − 10 = 16.

RP67 — B7: the tangent-perpendicular-to-radius theorem is unstated off-sheet recall, chained through an inverse Pythagorean step and a segment subtraction — a three-link chain with a circle-theorem gate (B7 descriptor: tangents, radii).
Problem 9 Band B7Student-Produced Response 3-D composite volume — inverse solve

The grain silo shown is formed by a right circular cylinder and a hemisphere on top. The cylinder and the hemisphere both have a radius of 6 meters. If the total volume of the silo is 864π cubic meters, what is the value of h, the height, in meters, of the cylindrical part?

Enter your answer in the grid.

Answer & Solution

Answer: 20

Cylinder volume: π(6²)h = 36πh. Hemisphere volume: ½ · (4/3)π(6³) = (2/3)π(216) = 144π. So 36πh + 144π = 864π, which gives 36h = 720 and h = 20 meters.

RP67 — B7: sheet formulas, but the item requires decomposing a composite solid, halving the sphere formula, and solving inversely for a dimension from total volume — a long inverse chain (B7 modeling descriptor).
Problem 10 Band B7Multiple Choice Similarity → trigonometric ratio (altitude to hypotenuse)

In the figure, triangle ABC has a right angle at C, and D lies on side AB such that segment CD is perpendicular to segment AB. If AD = 9 and DB = 16, what is the value of cos B?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

  1. A)3/5
  2. B)16/25
  3. C)3/4
  4. D)4/5
Answer & Solution

Answer: D) 4/5

The altitude to the hypotenuse creates triangles ACD, CBD, and ABC, all similar to one another. By the geometric-mean relation, CD² = AD · DB = 9 · 16 = 144, so CD = 12. In right triangle CDB: BC = √(12² + 16²) = √400 = 20. With AB = 9 + 16 = 25, cos B = BC/AB = 20/25 = 4/5. (Check inside triangle CDB: cos B = DB/BC = 16/20 = 4/5. ✓)

Distractors: A) is sin B (= cos A); B) uses the segment DB over AB, 16/25 (which equals cos²B — the similarity trap); C) is tan B (= 15/20).

RP67 — B7: the similarity structure of the altitude-on-hypotenuse configuration is unstated off-sheet recall, chained through a geometric-mean step, a Pythagorean step, and a trig-ratio read-off — the B7 "use similarity to calculate values of trigonometric ratios" descriptor.
