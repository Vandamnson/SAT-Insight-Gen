1.What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the right rectangular prism shown?

  1. A)16
  2. B)40
  3. C)120
  4. D)158
Answer & solution

Answer: C) 120

V = lwh = 8 · 5 · 3 = 120 cubic centimeters.

Band B4MCQ Triangle angle sum with a cevian

4.In the figure, point D lies on side BC of triangle ABC. What is the value of x?

  1. A)60
  2. B)75
  3. C)100
  4. D)105
Answer & solution

Answer: A) 60

Angle BAC = 35° + 45° = 80°. In triangle ABC: x = 180 − 40 − 80 = 60. (Cross-check through the cevian: ∠ADB = 180 − 40 − 35 = 105°, so ∠ADC = 75°, and x = 180 − 75 − 45 = 60. ✓)

Band B5MCQ Equation of a circle in the xy-plane

5.A circle in the xy-plane is shown. Which of the following is an equation of the circle?

  1. A)(x − 3)² + (y + 4)² = 25
  2. B)(x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 25
  3. C)(x − 3)² + (y + 4)² = 5
  4. D)(x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 5
Answer & solution

Answer: B) (x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 25

Center (−3, 4); radius from center to (1, 1): r² = (1 − (−3))² + (1 − 4)² = 16 + 9 = 25, so r = 5. Equation: (x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 25.

Distractors: A) sign-flips the center; D) uses r instead of r²; C) makes both errors.

B5 / RP67: off-sheet recall of the circle-equation form plus a distance computation for r² — a two-step forward chain; matches the B5 descriptor verbatim (center + point on circle given).

Band B6MCQ Triangle similarity — segment parallel to a side

7.In triangle ABC shown, segment DE is parallel to side BC. What is the length of BC?

  1. A)4
  2. B)11
  3. C)12
  4. D)16
Answer & solution

Answer: C) 12

Since DE ∥ BC, triangle ADE ~ triangle ABC. The ratio uses the whole side: AD/AB = 6/(6+3) = 2/3. So DE/BC = 2/3, giving BC = 8 · 3/2 = 12.

Distractors: A) sets DE/BC = AD/DB inverted (8 · 3/6); B) adds DB to DE (8 + 3); D) uses the part-to-part ratio AD/DB as the scale factor (8 · 6/3).

B6 / RP67: similarity with the part-whole trap — the solver must reject the tempting AD/DB proportion, derive AB = AD + DB, then set the correct correspondence; that non-obvious setup step is what lifts it past B4’s direct-scale-factor items.

Band B7MCQ Inscribed angle → arc length

8.In the circle shown, O is the center, and points A, B, and C lie on the circle. The radius of the circle is 18. What is the length of minor arc AB?

  1. A)7π/2
  2. B)7π
  3. C)14π
  4. D)63π
Answer & solution

Answer: B) 7π

The inscribed angle ∠ACB = 35° subtends arc AB, so the central angle for arc AB is 2 · 35° = 70°. Arc length = (70/360) · 2π(18) = (7/36) · 36π = 7π.

Distractors: A) forgets to double the inscribed angle (uses 35°); C) doubles twice (uses 140°); D) computes the sector area, (70/360)π(18²) = 63π.

B7 / RP67: off-sheet inscribed-angle theorem chained into the arc-length proportion — a two-theorem chain on circle parts; arcs/arc length live in the B7 descriptor.

Band B7SPR Similarity of right triangles → trig ratio

9.In the figure, ∠ACB is a right angle, and CD is perpendicular to AB. What is the value of tan A?

Student-produced response — enter your answer.

Answer & solution

Answer: 4/3 (equivalently 1.333)

Triangles ACD and CBD are similar (each is similar to triangle ACB), so CD/AD = DB/CD, giving CD² = 9 · 16 = 144 and CD = 12. In right triangle ACD, tan A = CD/AD = 12/9 = 4/3.

Cross-check in the big triangle: AC = √(9 · 25) = 15, BC = √(16 · 25) = 20, tan A = BC/AC = 20/15 = 4/3. ✓

B7 / RP67: matches B7’s “use similarity to calculate values of trigonometric ratios” verbatim — an off-sheet geometric-mean similarity relation must be found before any trig ratio can be formed (3-step chain, nothing direct on the sheet).
