B1 Multiple Choice Volume · right rectangular prism

The right rectangular prism shown has the dimensions, in centimeters, labeled in the figure. What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the prism?

  1. A16
  2. B64
  3. C120
  4. D158
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — 120

RP67 · Band 1. Forward one-step substitution into the reference-sheet formula V = ℓwh with all three edges handed over in the figure — the defining B1 skill.

V = ℓwh = 8 · 5 · 3 = 120 cubic centimeters. The three labels live only in the figure, so the figure is required.

Distractors: A = 8 + 5 + 3 (adds the edges); B = 4(8 + 5 + 3) = 64 (total edge length); D = 158 (computes surface area instead of volume).

B2 Multiple Choice Parallel lines · transversal

In the figure, line ℓ is parallel to line m, and line t intersects both lines. What is the value of x?

  1. A28
  2. B62
  3. C118
  4. D242
Answer & Solution

Answer: B — 62

RP67 · Band 2. One drilled parallel-lines theorem (same-side interior angles are supplementary), forward chain of length 1 — B2’s named skill, no reference-sheet support needed.

The 118° angle and the x° angle are same-side (co-interior) angles between the parallel lines, so they are supplementary: x = 180 − 118 = 62. Equivalently, the corresponding angle at line m is 118°, and x° forms a linear pair with it. The angle positions exist only in the figure.

Distractors: C treats the pair as congruent (alternate-interior confusion); A subtracts from 90 instead of 180; D reflexes to 360 − 118.

B4 Multiple Choice Similarity · scale factor

In triangle ABC shown, segment DE is parallel to segment BC. What is the length of BC?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

  1. A6
  2. B13
  3. C15
  4. D22.5
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — 15

RP67 · Band 4. A single similarity setup, but gated by the off-sheet part-to-whole ratio decision (AD:AB, not AD:DB) — exactly B4’s scale-factor/similarity skill.

Since DE ∥ BC, △ADE ~ △ABC (AA). The scale factor is AD/AB = 6/(6 + 4) = 3/5. So BC = DE ÷ (3/5) = 9 · (10/6) = 15. The segment lengths 6, 4, and 9 appear only in the figure.

Distractors: A uses the ratio AD:DB = 6:4 downward (9 · 4/6); B adds 9 + 4; D anchors the ratio on DB (9 · 10/4).

B5 Multiple Choice Circle equation · xy-plane

The graph of a circle in the xy-plane is shown. Which of the following equations defines the circle?

  1. A(x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 25
  2. B(x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 5
  3. C(x + 3)² + (y − 1)² = 25
  4. D(x + 3)² + (y − 1)² = 5
Answer & Solution

Answer: A — (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 25

RP67 · Band 5. Off-sheet standard form of a circle plus an embedded distance computation from a graphed point — a two-link chain matching B5’s named skill (equation from center + point on circle).

From the graph, the center is (3, −1) and the circle passes through (7, 2). Then r² = (7 − 3)² + (2 − (−1))² = 16 + 9 = 25, so the equation is (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 25. Both points must be read off the figure.

Distractors: B stops at r = 5 without squaring; C flips the center signs; D commits both errors.

B6 Multiple Choice Prism · volume → surface area

The right rectangular prism shown has a volume of 300 cubic inches. What is the surface area, in square inches, of the prism?

  1. A180
  2. B230
  3. C280
  4. D300
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — 280

RP67 · Band 6. Inverse gate (recover w from the volume) chained into an off-sheet six-face surface-area assembly — B6’s named prism skill (find a dimension, then another measure, of a prism).

Volume: w · 2w · 6 = 12w² = 300, so w² = 25 and w = 5. The prism is 5 × 10 × 6. Surface area = 2(5·10 + 5·6 + 10·6) = 2(50 + 30 + 60) = 280. The relationship between the base edges (w and 2w) is given only by the figure.

Distractors: A = 180 is the lateral area only (forgets top and bottom); B = 230 adds back just one 5 × 10 base; D echoes the given volume.

B7 Multiple Choice Circle theorems · tangents & arcs

In the figure, segments PA and PB are tangent to the circle at points A and B, respectively, and O is the center of the circle. What is the measure, in degrees, of minor arc AB?

  1. A42
  2. B84
  3. C138
  4. D222
Answer & Solution

Answer: C — 138

RP67 · Band 7. Three off-sheet links with zero reference-sheet support: tangent ⊥ radius, quadrilateral angle sum, then central-angle ↔ arc equivalence — B7’s circle-theorem skill.

Radii to points of tangency are perpendicular to the tangents, so ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°. In quadrilateral OAPB: ∠AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 42° = 138°. A central angle equals its intercepted arc, so minor arc AB = 138°. The tangency configuration is carried entirely by the figure.

Distractors: A transfers 42° straight to the arc; B doubles it as if 42° were an inscribed angle; D is the major arc, 360 − 138.

B7 Student-Produced Response Similar right triangles · altitude

In right triangle ABC shown, angle ACB is a right angle, and segment CD is perpendicular to hypotenuse AB. What is the length of segment CD?

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

Grid your answer

Answer & Solution

Answer: 12

RP67 · Band 7. Hidden double similarity (the altitude creates three similar triangles) extracted as a geometric mean — an off-sheet, multi-triangle chain squarely in B7’s similar-right-triangles skill.

△ACD ~ △CBD (each is similar to △ABC), so CD/AD = DB/CD, giving CD² = AD · DB = 9 · 16 = 144, so CD = 12.

Independent check: AC² = AD · AB = 9 · 25 = 225 → AC = 15; then CD = √(15² − 9²) = √144 = 12. ✓ (And 15-20-25 confirms the right angle at C.) The 9/16 split of the hypotenuse exists only in the figure.

B7 Student-Produced Response Composite solid · cylinder + cone

The solid shown consists of a right circular cylinder and a right circular cone that share a base. The dimensions shown are in centimeters. If the volume of the solid is 87π cubic centimeters, what is the value of h?

Grid your answer

Answer & Solution

Answer: 5

RP67 · Band 7. A composite-solid model: two reference-sheet formulas must be assembled, equated to a total, and solved inversely for a dimension — chain length 3, B7’s modeling gate.

Cylinder: π(3²)(8) = 72π. Cone: ⅓π(3²)h = 3πh. Total: 72π + 3πh = 87π → 3h = 15 → h = 5.

Check: 72π + 3π(5) = 87π. ✓ Which height belongs to which piece — and that the two pieces share the radius-3 base — is only decodable from the figure.
